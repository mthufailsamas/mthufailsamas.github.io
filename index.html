AI automation & document intelligence | n8n, Gemini, Python/FastAPI, PostgreSQL, Docker

End-to-End AI Invoice Approval Automation

Problem
Finance teams lose time and introduce avoidable errors when invoices arrive in different layouts and must be re-entered before totals, references, duplicates, and approval rules can be checked.
My Contribution
Designed and implemented the workflow end to end: multi-file intake, three n8n automations, Gemini extraction, a Python/FastAPI validation service, PostgreSQL workflow history, human review, and duplicate-safe delivery to an ERP sandbox.
Technical details
Data & Context
The interface accepts PDF, PNG, and JPEG vendor invoices in batches of up to 20 files. Each document keeps its own source identity, processing state, and audit history.
Approach

Validation & Controls

02

AI/ML engineering & retail forecasting | Python, XGBoost, FastAPI, Docker

Retail Sales Forecasting & Planning System

Problem
Retail planning requires a consistent 16-day view of category demand across 54 stores and 33 product families, while promotions, holidays, transactions, and local operating conditions vary across 1,782 store-family series.
My Contribution
Built the end-to-end system from multi-source preprocessing and forecast-available feature engineering through chronological Ridge/XGBoost evaluation, a versioned model artifact, reusable batch inference, and locally verified FastAPI and Docker delivery.
Technical details
Data & Context
Used 3,000,888 labeled rows from the Kaggle Store Sales competition covering 2013–2017. The prediction contract produces one forecast for every date, store, and product-family combination over the next 16 days.
Approach
Compared three Ridge settings with 27 XGBoost configurations on a fixed chronological validation window. XGBoost was selected by RMSLE before the frozen configuration was evaluated once on the protected internal-test window.
Validation & Delivery
The selected model recorded internal-test RMSLE of 0.4140 and WAPE of 15.6431%. Its versioned artifact generated 28,512 forecasts, while fresh-process batch, local FastAPI, and healthy Docker inference matched the notebook output. All 38 synthetic contract tests also passed in GitHub Actions.

Project OutputThe system produces a checked 16-day forecast for every store-family series, giving planning teams one consistent demand view through the same reusable artifact across batch, API, and containerized inference.

03

Time-series forecasting | Python, XGBoost, scikit-learn

Rainfall Forecasting with XGBoost and Grid Search

Problem
Daily rainfall is highly variable, and random train-test splits can overstate how well a model will perform on future weather. This project estimates next-day rainfall while keeping evaluation in chronological order.
My Contribution
Built the Python forecasting workflow from data validation and lag construction through XGBoost search, scikit-learn temporal evaluation, and experiment reporting, then served as first author of the resulting peer-reviewed publication.
Technical details
Data & Context
Daily BMKG observations from the Trunojoyo observation area in Sumenep Regency combine recent temperature, sunshine, humidity, wind, and rainfall history.
Approach
Created one-day lagged predictors and searched 243 XGBoost configurations, with Min-Max scaling fitted separately inside each training fold.
Validation & Controls
Used 10-fold TimeSeriesSplit and pooled out-of-fold predictions so every test window followed its training data and future observations did not leak into preprocessing.

11.9566RMSE (mm)
10Temporal Folds

Project OutputThe workflow delivers a selected XGBoost model, fold-level results, pooled out-of-fold rainfall predictions, and a traceable experiment report rather than a single unsupported accuracy figure.

Evidence & ScopeThe work became a first-author peer-reviewed publication. Original BMKG observations remain restricted, so the public repository provides synthetic same-schema data for reviewing and reproducing the workflow.

06

Deep learning & time-series forecasting | Python, TensorFlow/Keras, NumPy, Pandas

Rainfall Forecasting with BiLSTM and Grid Search

Problem
Next-day rainfall depends on changing weather patterns across several recent days, making a sequence-based model more appropriate than treating each observation as an isolated row.
My Contribution
Developed the Python forecasting workflow during my BMKG internship, using NumPy and Pandas for seven-day sequence preparation and TensorFlow/Keras for three-layer BiLSTM training, focused Grid Search, chronological evaluation, and result reporting.
Technical details
Data & Context
Daily BMKG observations from the Trunojoyo observation area in Sumenep Regency provide four weather variables and historical rainfall for each input sequence.
Approach
Built seven-day multivariate sequences, fitted Min-Max normalization on the training period, and trained a three-layer stacked BiLSTM while tuning units, batch size, and the learning-rate drop period.
Validation & Controls
Used an 80:20 chronological split so every evaluation observation followed the training period. Predictions were returned to millimeters and impossible negative rainfall values were clipped to zero.
0.8073MAAPE
10.2734 mmRMSE
7 daysInput Sequence

Project OutputThe workflow turns seven days of weather history into next-day rainfall estimates and preserves the selected model, holdout predictions, evaluation summaries, and actual-versus-predicted visual for review.

Evidence & ScopeThe reported BMKG internship results come from one chronological holdout period using restricted observations. The public repository includes synthetic same-schema data so the full workflow can still be inspected and run.

07

Statistical time-series forecasting | Python, statsmodels, Pandas

Rice Price Forecasting with ARIMA and Walk-Forward Validation

Problem
Price planning benefits from a forecast that is easy to explain and tested in the same order that future monthly prices would actually become available.
My Contribution
Implemented the Python/statsmodels ARIMA workflow for this collaborative academic project, covering Pandas-based monthly preparation, stationarity checks, order selection, diagnostic screening, expanding-window evaluation, and forecast reporting.
Technical details
Data & Context
Sixty public monthly observations from January 2020 through December 2024 cover medium and premium rice prices from the Open Data Jawa Timur portal.
Approach
Modeled medium and premium prices separately with ARIMA, using ADF-based differencing, ACF and PACF inspection, candidate evaluation, convergence checks, and residual diagnostics.
Validation & Controls
Started with the first 80% of observations, forecast the next month, added the newly available actual price, and repeated the process across the remaining 12 months.
2.08%Medium Rice MAPE
1.82%Premium Rice MAPE
12 monthsWalk-Forward Test

Project OutputThe workflow turns public monthly price history into explainable one-step-ahead forecasts, selected ARIMA orders, diagnostic records, error summaries, and comparison visuals for both rice categories.

Evidence & ScopeThe public data and runnable workflow are included in the repository. The models tracked the evaluated period closely, but rely only on past prices and do not include market events, policy changes, supply, or weather.
